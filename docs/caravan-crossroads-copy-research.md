# Caravan Crossroads copy and source research

Reviewed: 2026-11-02

## Lesson question

When every other rule stays fixed, how can one modeled route or season change
alter travel time, delivered cargo, load and margin?

The lesson uses two prediction-before-evidence investigations. The history
framing establishes that the Silk Roads were many connected routes and
communities; it does not present the fictional journey model as a
reconstruction.

## Institutional sources opened

### UNESCO — About the Silk Roads

URL:
https://www.unesco.org/en/silk-roads/about-silk-roads?hub=196704

The page describes the Silk Roads as networks of land and maritime routes. It
explains that goods as well as knowledge, ideas, cultures and beliefs moved
through exchanges among populations, and that individual routes joined and
crossed rather than forming one fixed itinerary. Its discussion of local trade
and caravanserais also supports naming intermediate communities and exchange
places instead of depicting a direct one-way line.

Implemented mapping:

- The registry explanation and every platform's model note say “overlapping
  land and sea networks” and “many communities in many directions.”
- The central relief map retains multiple intersecting routes, two markets, a
  water stop and intermediate exchange stops.
- The source does not validate any duration, toll, season effect, loss, load or
  price used by the lesson.

### UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Silk Roads: the Routes Network of

Chang'an–Tianshan Corridor

URL:
https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1442

The property description presents a long-distance network connecting multiple
civilizations across varied geographic regions. It identifies capital and
palace complexes, trading settlements, Buddhist cave temples, ancient paths,
posthouses, passes, beacon towers, fortifications, tombs and water-management
systems as parts of the corridor. This supports a network-and-communities
framing rather than a road-only or merchant-only account.

Implemented mapping:

- The visible history note says the activity shows one fictional composite
  segment and is not a reconstruction of a place, people or century.
- The scene shows terrain, river, markets, exchange places and a water stop as
  relevant parts of a route system, while the controlled evidence remains
  about the explicitly invented model.

### The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Afghanistan: Hidden Treasures from the

National Museum, Kabul

URL:
https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2009/afghanistan

The exhibition page explains that the Silk Road was not one road but a
collection of land and sea routes. It also states that products were seldom
carried end to end by the same merchants and instead moved hand to hand and
town to town. This is the most direct support for correcting the one-road,
one-way, single-merchant misconception.

Implemented mapping:

- The registry misconception explicitly rejects a simple east-to-west line
  and a single merchant carrying goods end to end.
- Web, native and package notes say goods often changed hands between
  merchants.

### British Museum — Silk Roads exhibition guide

URL:
https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/Silk_Roads.pdf

The guide describes overlapping networks that connected communities across
Asia, Africa and Europe and cautions against reducing the Silk Roads to a
simple East–West story. It supports the lesson's plural, multi-community
language and its removal of a one-direction route narrative.

Implemented mapping:

- The registry explanation, misconception and all platform notes use plural
  “networks,” “routes” and “communities.”
- No lesson copy claims that the fictional market names or model journey
  represent any place in the guide.

## Teaching-model assumptions

No institutional source above is used to validate the simulation's numbers.
The following are deliberately invented, deterministic teaching assumptions:

- fictional Willow market, Harbor town, exchange stops, route names and
  composite segment;
- 8, 9, 11, 12, 13 and 14-day route/season durations;
- a 100 kg capacity, 2 kg per supply day and one teaching credit per supply
  day;
- all cargo weights, purchase values, sale values and tolls;
- 25% grain spoilage after 10 modeled days and 15% Mountain Pass pottery
  breakage, each rounded up;
- all resulting costs, revenues, margins and delivery counts.

The model note visible on web, native and package surfaces calls these
assumptions—not historical measurements, probabilities or forecasts.

## Controlled investigation mapping

### 1. Change only the season

River Road, 6 grain units, 14 supply days, the 100 kg limit, and every price and
loss rule remain fixed. Wet season is the baseline; dry season is the
comparison. The model changes 8 to 14 travel days, 6 to 4 delivered units, and
margin −8 to −22 teaching credits while the 64 kg load remains fixed.

### 2. Change only the route

Dry season, 6 pottery units, 12 supply days, the 100 kg limit, and every price
and loss rule remain fixed. Mountain Pass is the baseline; Oasis Trail is the
comparison. The model changes 9 to 12 travel days, 5 to 6 delivered units,
toll 8 to 3 and margin 0 to 15 teaching credits while the 66 kg load remains
fixed.

Both investigations require a prediction before the comparison. Feedback
reports whether evidence matched, then states the baseline, comparison,
changed variable, held variables and model rule that produced the difference.
Changing a prediction clears its prior evidence and relocks free exploration.

## Editorial decisions

- Copy says “modeled,” “fictional,” “invented” or “teaching credits” wherever
  a learner could mistake a numeric result for history or forecasting.
- The historical correction remains visible in the lesson instead of being
  left only in the source list.
- The art keeps multiple routes and communities; selection is shown by line
  weight, light markers and text, not color alone.
- The scene description names the selected route, season, travel days,
  delivered units and load for nonvisual access.
- Free controls appear only after both investigations have evidence. They
  support further one-change comparisons without replacing the required
  prediction cycle.
- Reset returns the wet River Road baseline, clears both predictions and both
  feedback blocks, and relocks free exploration.
- Static state changes are instructionally sufficient, so the lesson adds no
  ornamental continuous animation. Camera movement remains optional and
  user-controlled in the dimensional renderer.

## Platform and visual boundary

Web and the generated native offline atlas use the shared dimensional relief
scene. Native platform controls provide a simpler accessible diagram alongside
the same lesson contract. The downloadable package owns a sandbox-safe bounded
model and scene copy; direct parity tests compare its calculations, invalid
inputs and scene signature with shared code. The surfaces are instructionally
equivalent, not claimed to be pixel-identical.
