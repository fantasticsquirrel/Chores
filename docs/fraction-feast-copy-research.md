# Fraction Feast copy and research review

Reviewed: 2026-11-02

## Teaching claims retained

- A fraction model is valid only when the named whole is consistent and its
  denominator counts equal-area parts of that whole.
- Splitting every part by the same factor changes both piece count and piece
  size without changing the selected amount: `1/2 = 2/4`.
- A larger denominator does not by itself mean a larger amount. With the whole
  held fixed, more equal parts means each individual part is smaller.
- Unlike fractions must be renamed in a common unit before their numerators are
  added. For the fixed investigation, `2/3 = 8/12`, `3/4 = 9/12`, and
  `8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 whole and 5/12`.

## Sources actually reviewed

1. Common Core State Standards Initiative, _Standards for Mathematical
   Practice and Content for Mathematics_ (fractions section, especially the
   same-whole condition, equal parts, equivalence, and addition as joining
   parts of the same whole):
   https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf
2. OpenStax, _Prealgebra 2e_, 4.1 “Visualize Fractions” (equal parts of a whole
   and the equivalent-fractions property):
   https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-1-visualize-fractions
3. OpenStax, _Prealgebra 2e_, 4.5 “Add and Subtract Fractions with Different
   Denominators” (least common denominators, equivalent forms, and adding
   numerators while keeping the shared denominator):
   https://openstax.org/books/prealgebra-2e/pages/4-5-add-and-subtract-fractions-with-different-denominators

The first source supports the lesson sequence and same-whole caveat. The two
OpenStax sections support the exact equivalence and common-denominator
operations shown in the feedback. No source was used to justify pizza styling;
the pizza is a manipulable area model, not scientific evidence.

## Editorial decisions

- The lesson asks for a prediction before either result can be revealed.
- Feedback names changed and held variables instead of merely announcing a
  correct answer.
- “Pieces” and “amount” are kept distinct in prompts, readouts, and controls.
- The exact common-denominator work remains visible even when a sum can be
  simplified, because the shared unit is the evidence for addition.
- Decorative labels and mood copy were removed. The remaining headings,
  instructions, evidence, reset, model boundary, and Sources content each serve
  the learning task.
